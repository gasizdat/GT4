Biography

Born Patrick Brunty, the eldest of ten children of a County Down farm labourer. He opened a school at sixteen, and in 1802 travelled to St John's College, Cambridge, where he entered his name in the new spelling he kept for the rest of his life.

Ordained in 1806, he served curacies in Essex, Shropshire and the West Riding before being appointed perpetual curate of Haworth in 1820. He held the living for forty-one years.

He was in print long before any of his children were, with Cottage Poems (1811) and The Rural Minstrel (1813). In 1846 Charlotte took him to Manchester for cataract surgery, and began Jane Eyre in the lodgings while he recovered.

He outlived his wife and all six of their children.
